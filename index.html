How Run in Chat works

  1. Fill in the Pin Intake Form, or a solo agent form, and add it to the Queue.
  2. Open the Queue and tap Run in Chat on that run. That sets the run to working and opens its Run in Chat card.
  3. Tap Copy Prompt on the Run in Chat card and paste it into chat with Indigo.
  4. Indigo runs that exact run in chat - nothing swapped - and brings the finished pin package back to you in chat for your approval.
  5. Back here, set the status yourself when you are ready: queued, working, or done. The status button only changes the label - it never executes anything. Execution happens in chat.

There is no scheduled pickup. Nothing runs on its own and nothing is posted automatically.

The Pin Standing Order

"A Pin is not done until it has a keyword title, a board, and a destination link." That order is baked into every run prompt and into the Pin Copy Agent's solo form. A pretty picture with no title, no board, and nowhere to send people is not a finished pin.

Connect Muse to Pinterest — Do This First

Want Muse to set up and run your Pinterest for you? There is no Pinterest connector button inside Muse — and you do not need one. The connection is Muse's built-in browser: Muse opens Pinterest in a live browser right inside your chat, you sign in yourself, and from then on Muse can work your Pinterest while you watch. Here is the whole setup:

  1. Start in Muse (the Muse app or muse.ai). Paste the starter prompt below into a new chat and send it.
  2. Muse opens Pinterest in its browser. A browser panel appears in your chat showing the live Pinterest page.
  3. YOU sign in — never Muse. Tap the browser panel, take control, and type your own Pinterest email and password on Pinterest's real login page. If Pinterest asks for a code or a passkey, you enter that in the browser too. Never type your password or a code into the chat itself.
  4. Hand it back and say "I'm in." Muse picks up right where you left off, confirms it can see your account, and gets to work.
  5. Stay the boss. You can watch every click in the browser panel, and Muse asks for your OK before anything is published. Scheduling a Pin is not posting it — scheduled Pins sit in your Scheduled list until their day comes.

Copy-ready starter prompt — paste this into Muse:

Act as my Pinterest marketing strategist. Open Pinterest in your browser — I will sign in myself when you show me the login page. Once I say I'm in, audit my profile, my boards, my settings and my analytics, and tell me what needs fixing BEFORE you change anything. My goal is outbound clicks and sales.

Once you are connected, Muse can: audit your profile, boards and analytics • clean up settings and boards with your OK • build your boards and claim your website • schedule Pins in Pinterest's own scheduler — up to 30 days ahead (times land on the hour and half hour) • check that every Pin carries a keyword title, a board, and a working destination link • re-check your clicks weekly so you can see the number move.

Safety, plain and simple: your password only ever goes into Pinterest's own login page inside the browser — never into chat, never into this dashboard. This dashboard stores nothing about your Pinterest account. You can sign out of Pinterest in that browser at any time and the connection ends.

Scene Sets — One Product, Four Pins

The Image Agent's 4-shot workflow. Give a run a person reference and a product reference, flip Scene Set on in the Intake form (or in the Image Agent's solo form), and the run produces four standalone images - each on its own canvas, never a grid or collage - planned as one set: 1) Hero - she presents the product, packaging to camera. 2) Lifestyle - a natural moment holding or using it. 3) Detail - a close product view with her still visible and recognizable. 4) Storytelling - an alternate action or angle with clean caption-safe space. Four shots = four Pins, or the stills for a video Pin.

Reference locks come first. The person's face, complexion, hair, body proportions and wardrobe, and the product's shape, colors, packaging and label placement, are locked from the actual references before anything is generated - never invented, never slimmed, never swapped. Optional extra references can be added, each with one job only: a skin close-up for texture, a body reference for silhouette, a pose reference, a wardrobe reference.

Scenes: home / living room, kitchen, bathroom vanity, bedroom, home office, studio, cafe, retail aisle, gym, park or garden, city street, travel, unboxing tabletop, product demonstration, testimonial, beauty editorial - or your own custom spot. Pick up to four: one scene = four compositions in it; two scenes = two shots each; none picked = the agent chooses one believable setting and varies the compositions.

Realism rules: believable photographic skin - pores, fine hairs, natural texture, no airbrushed or waxy finish; concrete light direction and camera language; natural hands, grip, contact shadows and product scale; no added logos, captions, watermarks, or invented label copy or claims. Every finished shot is checked before delivery - identity match, product fidelity, hands and limbs, distinct composition - and delivered as an image-and-prompt pair, Shot 01 through Shot 04, each prompt standing alone.

Scene Sets shine in Product Spotlight mode and anywhere a character presents a real product. The full step-by-step workflow lives in the farm's master prompt, inside the Image Agent's instructions.

Product Presentation - one choice, never both

Every run picks one single choice, right after Mode: Wearing the product OR Holding the product. Never both.

Wearing run: every prompt ends with this exact sentence: "Wearing the product, not holding it."

Holding run: every prompt ends with this exact sentence: "Holding the product, not wearing it."

Never show her wearing an outfit while holding that same outfit on a hanger. Reject and regenerate any image that shows both. If a video needs both, use separate scenes - holding first, wearing later - never the same shot.

That one choice is saved on the run and shown on the Queue card and the Run in Chat card, with its exact prompt sentence.

The Workflow - written into every run prompt

  1. Scout Pinterest in guest view for this pillar: search terms and keywords, top boards, and seasonal trends worked 45 days ahead. When the run needs a character look, pick the outfit on Pinterest (one with an Amazon link when there is a product). Copy its picture.
  2. Lock the outfit IN CHAT, not in the video tool: using your locked character's reference photos, put that character in that exact outfit in chat first. Check face match and outfit match.
  3. Make the outfit reference sheet IN CHAT: from the approved outfit photo, one single 9:16 sheet with front, profile, and three-quarter shots of her in that outfit, all on the same photo. Save as PNG.
  4. Build the pin asset with the Consistent Character lock: Reference One is her headshot (Slot 1). Reference Two is the chat-made outfit reference sheet. NEVER leave Reference Two empty. No character bible is needed - the reference photos ARE the lock. Do not paste descriptions of her into scene prompts.
  5. Create the scenes from that lock, in script order, 9:16.
  6. The Pin Copy Agent finishes the package: keyword-rich Pin title, full Pin description, board pick from your board list, 3 to 5 keyword tags, and the destination link.
  7. Return the finished pin package IN CHAT for your approval. Never post, publish, or share it.

The Scene Prompt Shape - use this, and nothing else about her

"Use the reference images and keep the character perfectly consistent. [Scene action only.] [Spoken line, if any.] Wearing the product, not holding it."

For a Holding run, the last sentence swaps to: "Holding the product, not wearing it."

NEVER describe her in a scene prompt - no face, hair, skin, body, or outfit description. Describing her overrides the references and creates a different girl.

Character lock rules

  • Your characters are ultra realistic, with real skin texture and real hair texture. Every character is African American unless you named a specific existing character. Plus-size stays plus-size, never slimmed down.
  • Slot 1 is one headshot photo, 9:16. Slot 2 is ONE single 9:16 body reference sheet - front, three-quarter, left profile, and right side views of the same person, same outfit, same background, all on that one photo. Not separate photos.
  • A character is NOT locked until BOTH slots are filled.
  • Within one video, identity, clothing, location, and lighting stay frozen from the reference photos.
  • Your uploads are accepted as JPG or PNG, as you saved them. This dashboard never forces your uploads to PNG. If you upload a webp, it is rejected with a clear message: "JPG or PNG please."

Standing rules

  • Every image your AI creates is PNG, never webp. Your own uploaded Vault photos stay as you saved them (JPG or PNG).
  • Nothing is posted automatically, ever. Posting always needs your separate approval.
  • No price, discount, or sale claim in any pin title, description, or copy unless a live price was actually verified.
  • A Pin is not done until it has a keyword title, a board, and a destination link.
  • Voice and copy everywhere: personable, human, plain words. No em dashes - use hyphens instead.
  • Your characters and runs save in this browser on this device. Use Export Backup on the Queue page to keep a copy you can bring back with Import Backup.
